Stammering Awareness Day

By James Gartshore · 22 October 2026 · Health and wellbeing
See the old version of this poster Download the old version

Hi everyone, today is International Stammering Awareness Day, so I wanted to share some free tech that can make life a bit easier for anyone who stammers.

Voice assistants like Siri can be frustrating if they cut you off before you've finished speaking. On an iPhone, you can switch on Type to Siri, which lets you type your request instead of saying it. You'll find it in Settings, then Accessibility, then Siri. On newer iPhones there's also a setting called Listen for Atypical Speech, in the Accessibility settings, which helps Siri understand speech that's different, including stammering.

Phone calls can be one of the hardest things, especially when you're under time pressure. Relay UK is a free app where you can type what you want to say during a call, and a relay assistant speaks your words to the other person. It's designed for people who are deaf or have speech difficulties, and anyone who finds speaking on the phone hard can use it.

And it's worth remembering that more and more things can be done by message instead of a call. The NHS App lets you message your GP surgery at many practices, most bank apps have a chat or messaging option, and lots of companies offer web chat on their websites.

If you or someone you know stammers and would like to talk to people who understand, STAMMA, the British Stammering Association, has free advice and support at stamma.org.
