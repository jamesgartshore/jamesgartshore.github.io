Got an old phone or laptop in a drawer?

By James Gartshore · 14 October 2026 · Everyday tech

In short

  • Google Photos or iCloud: Back up your photos and contacts before you wipe anything. (Free on Android and iPhone)
  • Factory reset: Wipes everything. Sign out of your Google account or Find My iPhone first. (Built in: Settings > System or General > Reset)
  • Recycle Your Electricals: Type in your postcode to find your nearest free drop-off point. (recycleyourelectricals.org.uk)
  • National Device Bank: Donate a working device so it goes to someone who can't afford one. (Run by Good Things Foundation)

Hi everyone, today is International E-Waste Day, which is a good reminder about all those old phones, tablets and laptops sitting in drawers. Most of us have at least one, and a lot of people hang on to them because they're worried about their personal information or don't know what to do with them. There are some free tools that make it easy.

Before anything else, save your photos and contacts. Google Photos on Android and iCloud on iPhone will back them up for you, so they'll be there on your new phone too. You can also copy them onto a USB stick or another computer.

Every phone and laptop has a built-in factory reset, which wipes everything and puts it back to how it was when it was new. Before you use it, sign out of your accounts. On an iPhone, go to Settings, tap your name at the top and sign out, which also turns off Find My iPhone. On Android, go to Settings, then Accounts, and remove your Google account. If you skip this, the device stays locked to you and nobody else can use it. Then you'll find the reset in Settings under System or General, then Reset. On a Windows laptop, search Settings for "Reset this PC" and choose to remove everything. Don't forget to take out the SIM card and any memory card too.

Once it's wiped, the free Recycle Your Electricals website at recycleyourelectricals.org.uk lets you type in your postcode to find your nearest drop-off point, including shops that take old tech for free.

If it still works, you could donate it to the National Device Bank, run by the charity Good Things Foundation. They refurbish donated phones, tablets and laptops and pass them on to people who can't afford one.

Whatever you do, please don't put old phones, laptops or batteries in your household bin. The batteries inside can catch fire when they're crushed in bin lorries and at waste centres.

If you're not sure how to wipe your old device, just send me a message and I'll do my best to help.

I hope this helps, and feel free to pass it on to anyone who might find it useful.
