Facebook privacy and fake friends

In this lesson

  • How to make your posts friends-only
  • How to spot a copied profile
  • What to do about suspicious requests

Facebook posts are often set to Public, so anyone in the world can see them, including scammers. A couple of settings keep your posts between friends, and knowing the signs helps you spot fake friend requests.

Good to know

  • Public means anyone, even people not on Facebook. Friends is usually best.
  • A request from someone you're already friends with is almost always a copied profile. Delete it and tell your friend.
  • New profiles with few friends that ask for money or gift cards are scammers.
  • Marketplace buyers who want to pay "by courier" or ask for a code sent to your phone are scammers.

Try it yourself: tighten your privacy

Make your posts friends-only, then deal with a suspicious friend request. A practice app only.

The steps, written out

Facebook app: Menu > Settings & privacy > Settings > Audience and visibility > Posts > Who can see your future posts? > Friends. Friend requests are under the Friends tab.

Tip: Menu > Settings & privacy > Privacy Checkup walks you through the main settings.

Menu names can differ slightly between phone models and updates.

Remember

  • Set your posts to Friends, not Public.
  • A second request from an existing friend is a copy.
  • Delete it, and tell your real friend.
  • Never send money to someone you only know online.
