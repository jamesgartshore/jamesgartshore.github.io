Scam texts and emails

In this lesson

  • The scam texts and emails people see most
  • How to check a message safely, without clicking anything
  • Where to forward scams so they get blocked

Scam texts and emails are the ones people see most. More than half of people in the UK have had a fake parcel text, for example. They all work the same way: something worrying or tempting, a deadline, and a link to a copy of a real website that collects your details.

The ones to watch for

  • Parcel and delivery fees: "We missed you, pay £1.45 to rebook." Couriers don't text links asking you to pay.
  • Parking fines, tax refunds and benefits: fake messages from councils, HMRC or the DWP. They don't text you a link to pay a fine or claim money. The Winter Fuel Payment is paid automatically.
  • "Your account is suspended" from Netflix, Amazon, TV Licensing, your bank or email provider.
  • "Call us about this payment": a text that looks like your bank, with a number to ring. The number goes straight to the scammer.
  • "Hi Mum, this is my new number": a message pretending to be your child or grandchild, who soon needs money urgently.
  • "I sent you a code by mistake": a friend's hijacked WhatsApp asking for a 6-digit code. Passing it on hands over your account.

How to check a message safely

  • Don't use the link. Open the company's app, or type their website address yourself.
  • Don't call the number in the message. For your bank, call 159 or the number on the back of your card.
  • "Hi Mum" or a friend asking for help? Call them on the number you already have, or ask something only they'd know. Voice notes can be faked.
  • Never share a code sent to your phone. It's like the key to your account.

Report it, it's free

Scam text: forward it to 7726, then delete it. Your network can then block the sender.
Scam email: forward it to report@phishing.gov.uk, then delete it.
WhatsApp: open the chat and tap Block and Report.

Try it yourself: real or scam?

Look at each message and decide. You'll find out why straight away.

Message 1 of 6

EVRI
We missed your parcel today. A £1.45 redelivery fee is due. Pay now to rebook: evri-redelivery-uk.com/pay

Couriers don't text links asking for a fee, and that web address isn't the courier's. If you're expecting a parcel, check in the courier's app or the shop where you ordered.

Message 2 of 6

NHS
You have a new message from your GP surgery. Open the NHS App to read it.

This one is fine: there's no link, no deadline and nothing to pay. It just tells you to open the app yourself, which is the safe way to check anything.

Message 3 of 6

From HMRC <refunds@hmrc-tax-gov.com>
Subject You are due a tax refund of £326.40
Our records show you have overpaid tax. To receive your refund, confirm your bank details within 24 hours using the secure link below.

HMRC never emails or texts about refunds, and its real emails come from addresses ending in .gov.uk. The 24-hour deadline is there to rush you. Forward it to report@phishing.gov.uk.

Message 4 of 6

+44 7700 900461
Hi Mum, this is my new number, my phone fell in the bath! Can you message me on WhatsApp? Need a favour x

This is the "Hi Mum" scam. The "favour" soon becomes an urgent payment. Call your child or grandchild on the number you already have before doing anything.

Message 5 of 6

Barclays
A payment of £529.00 to CURRYS was attempted on your card ending 4421. If this wasn't you, call our fraud team now on 0161 496 0193.

Your bank does send alerts, which is exactly why scammers copy them. Don't call the number in the message. Call 159 or the number on the back of your card. If it's real, your bank will be able to see it.

Message 6 of 6

Sarah (friend)
Hiya! Sorry, I've sent you a 6-digit code by mistake. Can you send it back to me? x

Someone has taken over your friend's account. That code is the key to your WhatsApp, and sending it hands your account over. Never share a code, and let your friend know by phone.

Remember

  • Don't click links in unexpected texts or emails. Open the app or type the website address yourself.
  • Couriers, councils, HMRC and the DWP don't text you a link to pay a fee or claim a refund.
  • A "Hi Mum, new number" message is a scam until you've checked by calling their old number.
  • Never pass on a code that's sent to your phone, even to a friend.
  • Forward scam texts to 7726 and scam emails to report@phishing.gov.uk. Both are free.
