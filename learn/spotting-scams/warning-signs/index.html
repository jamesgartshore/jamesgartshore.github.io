The warning signs

In this lesson

  • The six warning signs almost every scam uses
  • Why a message can look real and still be a scam
  • The one rule that protects you from all of them

Scams arrive by text, email, phone call, social media and even letter. They change all the time, but almost all of them rely on the same few tricks. Once you know the tricks, you can spot scams you've never seen before.

The six warning signs

1

It rushes you

"Act now", "within 24 hours", "your account will be closed today". Scammers hurry you so you don't stop to think.

2

It scares or excites you

A fine, a debt or a problem with your bank. Or a prize, a refund or an amazing bargain. Strong feelings stop us thinking clearly.

3

It asks for details or codes

Your PIN, passwords, card details, or a code sent to your phone. Real banks and organisations never ask for these.

4

It wants an odd way to pay

Gift cards, moving money to a "safe account", cryptocurrency, or cash or cards handed to a courier. These always mean a scam.

5

It asks you to keep it secret

"Don't tell the bank staff", "don't tell your family". A real organisation will never ask you to keep things from people.

6

Something feels off

A link you weren't expecting, "Dear customer", or odd spelling. Many scams now look perfect, though, so don't rely on this one.

Looking real isn't proof

Scammers can make a call or text appear to come from a real number, and scam texts can even appear in the same conversation as genuine ones from your bank. Computers can now copy a person's voice. So "it came from their number" or "it sounded just like them" is no longer proof.

The one rule that always works

Stop. Don't reply, click, call back or pay. Then check for yourself, using a number or website you already know: the number on the back of your bank card, an official letter, or typing the website address yourself. A real organisation will never mind you doing this.

The UK's Take Five to Stop Fraud campaign puts it as Stop. Challenge. Protect.

    Remember

    • Rushing you, scaring you or offering something too good to be true are the biggest warning signs.
    • Real banks and organisations never ask for your PIN, passwords or the codes sent to your phone.
    • Gift cards, a "safe account" or a courier collecting cash or cards always mean a scam.
    • A real-looking number, text or even voice is not proof. They can all be faked.
    • Stop. Don't reply, click or pay. Then check for yourself using a number or website you already know.
