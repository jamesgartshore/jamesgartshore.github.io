If you think you've been scammed

In this lesson

  • What to do straight away if you think you've been scammed
  • Who to report it to, and where to get support
  • How to make your own scam emergency card

If you think you've been scammed, please don't be embarrassed. Scams are made by professional criminals and they fool clever, careful people every day. The most important thing is to act quickly, because the sooner your bank knows, the more it may be able to do.

What to do straight away

  1. Call your bank. Dial 159 or the number on the back of your card. Tell them what happened. They may be able to stop the payment or freeze your account.
  2. Report it. Contact Report Fraud (it used to be called Action Fraud) at reportfraud.police.uk or on 0300 123 2040. In Scotland, call Police Scotland on 101. If it's happening right now, for example a courier is on the way to your house, call 999.
  3. Change your passwords if you shared any, starting with your email and bank. If someone got onto your computer, have it checked before you use online banking again.
  4. Tell someone you trust. It helps to talk it through, and they can help you watch for follow-ups.

Watch out for "recovery" scams

People who've been scammed are sometimes contacted again by someone offering to get their money back for a fee. This is another scam. Report Fraud and your bank will never charge you.

Where to get support

  • Victim Support: free, confidential support on 08 08 16 89 111, whether or not you've reported it.
  • Citizens Advice consumer helpline: 0808 223 1133 for problems with something you bought.
  • More free help is on the Get help page.

Make your scam emergency card

Fill this in and print it, then keep it by your phone or in your purse or wallet. If something ever happens, you won't have to search for numbers while you're upset. Nothing you type here is sent anywhere or saved.

Remember

  • Don't be embarrassed. Scams fool clever, careful people every day.
  • Call your bank straight away on 159 or the number on your card. The sooner, the better.
  • Report it to Report Fraud (formerly Action Fraud): reportfraud.police.uk or 0300 123 2040.
  • If you shared a password or let someone onto your computer, change your passwords and get it checked.
  • Watch out for "recovery" scams offering to get your money back for a fee.
