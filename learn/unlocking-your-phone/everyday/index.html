Using them safely every day

In this lesson

  • What your face or fingerprint can approve, like downloads and payments
  • Why your phone sometimes asks for the passcode anyway
  • How to turn on extra protection in case your phone is stolen

Once it's set up, your face or fingerprint does more than unlock your phone. Here's what to expect day to day, and how to keep it safe.

What it's used for

  • Downloading apps: on an iPhone, double-click the side button and look at the screen. On Android you may be asked for your fingerprint or Google password.
  • Banking apps: most let you log in with your face or fingerprint instead of typing a password.
  • Paying in shops with Apple Pay or Google Wallet: your face or fingerprint approves each payment.

When it doesn't work

  • Your passcode always works, so you're never locked out just because it didn't recognise you.
  • After switching your phone off and on, it always asks for your passcode first. That's normal, and it's a safety feature.
  • Every few days it may ask for your passcode anyway, to make sure you haven't forgotten it.

Extra protection if your phone is stolen

iPhone: turn on Stolen Device Protection in Settings > Face ID & Passcode. Then, away from home, important changes need your face or fingerprint, not just your passcode, so a thief who has seen your code can't take over your phone.

Android: turn on Theft protection in Settings > Google > All services > Theft protection (on Samsung, Settings > Security and privacy > Lost device protection). It can lock your phone automatically if someone snatches it.

And make sure Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) is on, so you can find, lock or wipe a lost phone.

Try it yourself: what would you do?

Question 1 of 4

Someone calls saying they're from your bank. They ask you to read out your phone's passcode so they can "secure your account".

No bank, police force or phone company will ever ask for your phone's passcode. This is a scam. Hang up, and call your bank on 159 if you're worried.

Question 2 of 4

Your grandson asks to add his fingerprint to your phone so he can help you with it.

Any fingerprint or face that's added can unlock your phone and approve payments in your apps, whenever they like. It's safest to only add your own.

Question 3 of 4

You've just restarted your phone, and it won't unlock with your face. It asks for your passcode.

Phones always ask for the passcode after a restart. It's a safety feature, and face or fingerprint unlocking works again afterwards.

Question 4 of 4

You're paying at a busy shop till and need to type your passcode into your phone.

Thieves watch people type their passcode, then snatch the phone. Cover the screen, or use your face or fingerprint so you don't need to type it at all.

Remember

  • Your face or fingerprint can approve app downloads, banking log-ins and payments.
  • Phones always ask for the passcode after a restart. That's normal.
  • Turn on Stolen Device Protection (iPhone) or Theft protection (Android).
  • Make sure Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) is switched on.
