Touch ID and fingerprints

In this lesson

  • Where the fingerprint sensor is on different phones
  • How to add a fingerprint so it works reliably
  • Practise adding one on a pretend phone

A fingerprint is a quick and secure way to unlock your phone. On iPhones with a round Home button on the front, like the iPhone SE, it's called Touch ID. Most Android phones have a fingerprint sensor too, on the screen, the back, or the power button.

Tips for it to work well

  • Add the finger you naturally use when you pick up your phone. You can add more than one, and it's worth adding both thumbs.
  • Clean, dry fingers work best. Wet hands, cuts or very dry skin can stop it working for a while, and your passcode always works instead.
  • Moving the finger slightly each time during setup helps it learn the whole fingertip.
  • Only add your own fingers. Anyone whose fingerprint is added can unlock your phone and approve payments.

Try it yourself: add a fingerprint

Use the practice code when it asks, then tap the pretend sensor to "record" your fingerprint.

The steps, written out

iPhone with a Home button: Settings > Touch ID & Passcode > type your passcode > Add a Fingerprint. Lift and rest your finger on the Home button until it's complete, then do the same with the edges of your fingertip.

Android (Google Pixel): Settings > Security & privacy > Device unlock > Face & Fingerprint Unlock > type your PIN > Fingerprint Unlock > I agree. Touch the sensor, lift, and repeat until it's full.

Samsung: Settings > Security and privacy > Biometrics > Fingerprints.

Remember

  • Add the finger you naturally use when picking up your phone, and consider both thumbs.
  • Clean, dry fingers work best. Your passcode always works if it doesn't.
  • Only add your own fingerprints.
  • Samsung keeps fingerprints in Settings > Security and privacy > Biometrics.
