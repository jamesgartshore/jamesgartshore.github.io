Accessibility

I want everyone to be able to use this site, whatever their age, eyesight, hearing or confidence with technology. I aim to meet the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG 2.2) at level AA, the standard UK public sector websites follow.

Make the site easier to use

Tap Display options at the top of any page to change:

  • Text size: make all the writing bigger.
  • Colours: light, dark or high contrast (yellow and white on black).
  • Easy-read font: a font designed to make letters easier to tell apart.
  • More spacing: extra room between lines and paragraphs.
  • Underline links: so every link stands out.

Your choices are remembered on your device. The site also follows your phone or computer's own settings for text size, dark mode, high contrast and reduced motion, so if you've already set those, you don't need to do anything.

What else helps

  • Every tip has a Listen to this tip button that reads it out loud.
  • Everything on each poster is also written out in plain text on the page, in the "In short" list and the post below it, so screen readers and translation tools can read it.
  • Tips are written in plain English, with a Tech words page explaining common terms.
  • You can use the whole site with a keyboard, with a clear highlight showing where you are and a "Skip to content" link.
  • The site works on phones, tablets and computers, and you can zoom in up to 400% without losing anything.
  • Nothing moves, flashes or plays sound on its own, and there are no time limits.

What doesn't work as well yet

  • The posters themselves are pictures, so the writing on them can't be resized or read by a screen reader. The same information is always written out on the page.
  • Some links go to other websites, such as Nextdoor, follow.it and the services on the Get help page. I can't control how easy those sites are to use.
