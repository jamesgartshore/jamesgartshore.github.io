Media Literacy Week Day 5

By James Gartshore · 28 October 2026 · Fake news and online info
See the old version of this poster Download the old version

Hi everyone, it's day five of Global Media and Information Literacy Week, and today's tips are about WhatsApp forwards and chain messages.

We've all had them: "Forward this to everyone you know", warnings about a new virus, a dangerous product or a hacker, or a story that seems too shocking to be true. Many of these are false, out of date, or even scams, but because they come from friends and family, they're easy to believe.

WhatsApp helps you spot these. If a message has been passed on many times, it will show a "Forwarded many times" label with a double arrow at the top. That means it didn't come from the person who sent it to you, and it's been shared through a long chain of people. It's a good reason to stop and check before passing it on.

You can also choose who's allowed to add you to WhatsApp groups, which can cut down on unwanted messages. Go to Settings, then Privacy, then Groups, and choose My contacts or My contacts except.

If a group is busy and full of forwards, you don't have to leave it. Just press and hold the chat and tap Mute, and you'll stop getting notifications.

And if you get a message from someone you don't know sharing a scam or false information, open the chat, tap their name or number at the top, and choose Block or Report.

A message asking you to "forward this to 10 friends" is a big warning sign. If you're ever unsure, it's best not to pass it on.

If you've had a forward and you're not sure whether it's true, just send me a message and I'll do my best to help.
