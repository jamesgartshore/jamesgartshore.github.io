Help for local groups

Run a community group, club, church, hall or small charity? There are lots of free and discounted tools that can save you time and money and help you reach more people. Here's where to find them, and how I can help.

Free and discounted software

Most of these need you to be a registered charity, so check the rules for each one. Some are open to other not-for-profit groups too.

  • Google for Nonprofits: free Google Workspace (shared email, calendars and storage), plus free advertising on Google for eligible charities.
  • Microsoft for Nonprofits: free and discounted Microsoft 365, with Word, Excel, Outlook and Teams.
  • Canva for Nonprofits: the paid version of Canva free for eligible charities, for posters, leaflets and social media.

Getting found online

  • Google Business Profile: free. Puts your group, hall or venue on Google Maps and search, with your opening times, photos and directions.
  • Google Sites: a simple, free website. Fine for a few pages about your group.
  • A Facebook page or group: free, and where many local people already look for what's on.
  • Local directories: your council and local community websites often list groups for free. It's worth making sure yours is there and up to date.

Bookings, forms and keeping in touch

  • Google Forms or Microsoft Forms: free sign-up sheets, volunteer rotas, surveys and booking forms.
  • Eventbrite: free to use for free events, with reminders sent to people who book.
  • WhatsApp Communities: keep members updated with one announcement group, plus separate chats for teams or activities.
  • Email newsletters: Mailchimp and MailerLite both have free plans for small mailing lists.

Keeping your group safe online

Groups are targeted by scammers too, often with fake invoices or emails pretending to be the treasurer or chair.

  • The NCSC's Small Charity Guide: free, short and written for small groups by the UK's National Cyber Security Centre.
  • Turn on two-step verification for your group's email, social media and bank. It stops most account takeovers.
  • Don't share passwords in messages. A free password manager like Bitwarden lets trusted people share logins safely.
  • Keep a list of who can get into each account, and remove people when they step down.
  • Check any change of bank details by phone before paying, using a number you already have.
  • My free Spotting scams course works well for volunteers too.

Clear, easy-to-read materials

  • Plain English and big text: short sentences, at least 12 point in print, and dark text on a light background.
  • Word has a built-in checker: go to Review > Check Accessibility and it will point out things that are hard to read.
  • Describe your pictures when you post them online, so people using screen readers know what they show.
  • Posters sized properly for Facebook, Nextdoor or printing. I design these for free: see my design examples.

Helping your members get online

  • Good Things Foundation: groups can join its free National Digital Inclusion Network, and give out free mobile data SIM cards and refurbished devices to people who need them.
  • Learn My Way: free online courses for beginners, from using a mouse to staying safe online.
  • My free online courses and tech tips, which you're welcome to share or print.
  • Free drop-in tech help sessions where your members can get one-to-one help.

How I can help

I volunteer some of my spare time to help local groups make the most of these tools. I can help with things like:

  • Choosing and setting up free tools, such as shared email, booking forms or a Google listing
  • Making your group's accounts safer
  • A poster, leaflet or social media graphic
  • A friendly second opinion on your website or Facebook page
  • Ideas for helping your members get online

How it works

  • It's free. It's volunteering, so there's no charge.
  • Your group stays in control. I show you how and give advice; your group sets things up and keeps its own accounts and passwords. I'll never need your passwords.
  • A few groups at a time. I'll let you know honestly if I can't help straight away.
  • Local. Bridlington, Driffield and across the East Riding of Yorkshire, in person or online.
