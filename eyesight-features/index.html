Struggling to read your phone?

By James Gartshore · 14 October 2026 · Health and wellbeing

In short

  • Magnifier: Turns your camera into a magnifying glass for menus, labels and small print. (iPhone: Magnifier app; Android: Settings > Accessibility)
  • Bigger, bolder text: Makes everything on screen larger and easier to read. (Settings > Display, or Settings > Accessibility)
  • Screen reader: Reads out everything on the screen as you touch it. (iPhone: VoiceOver; Android: TalkBack)
  • Apps that describe: Point your camera at something and the app tells you what it is. (Free: Be My Eyes and Seeing AI)

Hi everyone, this week is National Braille Week, so I wanted to share some free features on your phone that can make a big difference if your eyesight isn't what it used to be.

One of the most useful is the magnifier, which turns your phone's camera into a magnifying glass. It's brilliant for reading menus, medicine labels, small print on letters, or anything else that's hard to see. You can zoom in, turn on the torch, and even freeze the picture so you can read it more easily. On an iPhone, there's an app called Magnifier already on your phone. On Android, you'll find it in Settings under Accessibility, or as a Magnifier app on some phones.

You can also make everything on your screen bigger and easier to read. Go into Settings, then Display or Accessibility, and look for the text size option. You can make the text larger and bolder, and on most phones you can zoom in on the whole screen too.

If reading the screen is difficult, a screen reader will read out everything on it as you touch it. On an iPhone it's called VoiceOver and on Android it's called TalkBack, both found in Settings under Accessibility. They work a bit differently to normal, so it's worth practising with them first.

There are also two free apps that can describe things for you. Be My Eyes connects you to friendly volunteers through a video call, who can tell you what they can see, like reading a label or checking the colour of something. It also has an AI option that can describe a photo straight away. Seeing AI, made by Microsoft, can read text out loud as soon as you point your camera at it, and can describe people, objects and scenes.

For more advice, the RNIB Helpline on 0303 123 9999 offers free help and information about sight loss, including using technology.
