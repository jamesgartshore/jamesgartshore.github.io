Is your sat nav out of date?

By James Gartshore · 08 October 2026 · Everyday tech

In short

  • Missing new roads: It doesn't know about a new road, bypass or roundabout.
  • Roads that are gone: It sends you down roads that are closed or no longer exist.
  • Arguing with the signs: Its route keeps disagreeing with the road signs.

Hi everyone, here's a quick tip for anyone who still uses a sat nav in the car.

Map apps on your phone, like Google Maps, update themselves automatically. A lot of standalone sat navs don't. They only get new maps when you connect them to a computer or Wi-Fi, so if yours hasn't been updated in a while it could be working from maps that are years old.

Signs that yours might be out of date include not knowing about a new road or roundabout, trying to send you down a road that's closed or no longer there, or taking you a different way from what the road signs say.

Updating is usually free and fairly simple. For a Garmin, install Garmin Express on a computer and plug the sat nav in with its USB cable. For a TomTom, it's the same idea using a program called MyDrive Connect. Newer models can often update over Wi-Fi from the settings menu instead. If your sat nav is built into your car, check the car maker's app or website, or ask your dealer.

Until it's updated, always go by the road signs if they disagree with your sat nav.

If you're not sure how to update yours, comment below and I'll do my best to help. Feel free to pass this on to anyone who might find it useful.
