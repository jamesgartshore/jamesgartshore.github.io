Got a text about a missed parcel?

By James Gartshore · 15 October 2026 · Scams and staying safe

In short

  • The courier's own app: Check your parcel in the official app, never through a link in a text. (Royal Mail, Evri, DPD and Amazon apps)
  • Spam filters: Moves texts from unknown numbers and likely scams out of your inbox. (Android: Messages > Spam protection; iPhone: Messages > Unknown Senders)
  • Forward to 7726: Report the scam text for free so the number can be blocked. (Works on all UK networks)
  • Website checker: Paste in a web address to see if it's safe before you open it. (Free at getsafeonline.org)

Hi everyone, I wanted to share a warning about fake parcel delivery texts, as they're one of the most common scams around at the moment.

They usually look something like this: "Royal Mail: your parcel could not be delivered. Please pay the £1.45 redelivery fee at this link." Others claim to be from Evri, DPD or Amazon, or say there's a customs charge to pay. The link takes you to a website that looks real, but it's designed to steal your card details. Because so many of us are waiting on a parcel at any one time, they can be very convincing.

The safest way to check is to use the courier's own app or website, rather than any link in a text. Royal Mail, Evri, DPD and Amazon all have free official apps where you can track your parcels. If there really is a problem with a delivery, it will show up there.

Your phone can also help keep these texts out of your inbox in the first place. On Android, open the Messages app, go to Settings and make sure Spam protection is switched on. On an iPhone, go to Settings, then Apps, then Messages, and switch on the option to filter Unknown Senders. Texts from numbers you don't know will then go into a separate list.

If you do get one, you can report it for free by forwarding it to 7726. This works on all UK networks and helps them block the number so it can't reach other people. Then just delete it.

And if you're ever unsure about a link, Get Safe Online has a free website checker at getsafeonline.org. Paste in the web address and it will tell you whether it looks safe.

The main thing to remember is to never pay a redelivery fee or enter your card details from a link in a text. If you think you might have already entered your details, contact your bank straight away by calling 159.

If you've had one of these texts and you're not sure whether it's real, just send me a message and I'll do my best to help.

I hope this helps, and feel free to pass it on to anyone who might find it useful.
