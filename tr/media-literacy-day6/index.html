Media Literacy Week Day 6

By James Gartshore · 29 October 2026 · Fake news and online info
See the old version of this poster Download the old version

Hi everyone, it's day six of Global Media and Information Literacy Week, and today I wanted to talk about health information online.

False health advice and so-called miracle cures spread very quickly online, often with dramatic stories or videos of people claiming amazing results. Some are harmless, but others can be dangerous, especially if they encourage people to stop taking their medication or to buy unregulated products.

The NHS website is the best place to start if you want to know more about a condition or treatment. Go to nhs.uk and look at the Health A to Z, which has clear, trusted information on hundreds of conditions, from symptoms to treatments.

If you're worried about symptoms, NHS 111 online at 111.nhs.uk asks you questions and tells you what to do next, whether that's looking after yourself at home, seeing a pharmacist, or getting urgent help. You can also call 111.

Your local pharmacist is another great source of free advice, especially about medicines and many common conditions. You can find your nearest pharmacy on the NHS website.

If you come across fake medicines being sold online, or you've had side effects from a medicine or product, you can report it through the Yellow Card scheme at yellowcard.mhra.gov.uk. It's run by the UK medicines regulator, and your reports help keep everyone safe.

Most importantly, never stop or change any medicine you've been prescribed because of something you've read or watched online. Always speak to your GP or pharmacist first.

If you've seen a health claim and you're not sure whether to trust it, just send me a message and I'll do my best to help you find reliable information.
