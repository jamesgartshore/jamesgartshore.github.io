Get Online Week

By James Gartshore · 19 October 2026 · Getting online and staying connected
See the old version of this poster Download the old version

Hi everyone, this week is Get Online Week, a national campaign to help more people feel confident using the internet. Being online has become part of everyday life, and it can save you a lot of time, money and frustration.

Take your health, for example. With the free NHS App, you can order repeat prescriptions, see your GP record and test results, and manage appointments, all without waiting on the phone first thing in the morning.

Banking is much easier too. Your bank's official app lets you check your balance, see what's gone out and pay bills at any time of day, without having to visit a branch, which is handy now so many have closed. Just make sure you download it from the App Store or Google Play and not from a link in a text or email.

Lots of government services are online as well, like checking your State Pension, renewing your driving licence or applying for a Blue Badge. Always use gov.uk, as it's the only official site. Some other sites look official but charge you extra for things that are free or cheaper on gov.uk.

And of course, being online makes it easy to stay in touch. Video calls on WhatsApp, FaceTime or Facebook Messenger are free, so you can see family and friends wherever they live.

If you don't have internet at home, there's still help available. Most libraries offer free use of computers and Wi-Fi, and the National Databank, run by the charity behind Get Online Week, gives free mobile data to people on low incomes. Your local library or community centre can often help you sign up.

If you'd like help getting started with any of this, just send me a message and I'll do my best to help.
