Всемирный день инсульта

By James Gartshore · 29 October 2026 · Health and wellbeing
See the old version of this poster Download the old version

Hi everyone, today is World Stroke Day, so I wanted to share how to spot the signs of a stroke, and how your phone can help in an emergency.

A stroke is a medical emergency, and the faster someone gets help, the better their chance of recovery. The easiest way to remember the signs is FAST. Face: has their face fallen on one side, or can they not smile? Arms: can they raise both arms and keep them there? Speech: is their speech slurred, or are they struggling to talk? Time: if you see any of these signs, call 999 straight away. Don't wait to see if it gets better.

Your phone can help you get help quickly. Most phones have an Emergency SOS feature. If you press the side or power button five times quickly, it will call 999 for you, and can also send your location to your emergency contacts. It's worth checking it's switched on, which you'll find in Settings under Emergency SOS on an iPhone, or Safety and emergency on Android.

It's also a good idea to fill in your Medical ID. This lets paramedics see important information like your medical conditions, the medicines you take and who to contact, even if your phone is locked. On an iPhone, open the Health app and tap Medical ID. On Android, go to Settings, then Safety and emergency, then Medical information.

For anyone recovering from a stroke, or caring for someone who is, the Stroke Association has a free online service called My Stroke Guide, with information, advice and a community of people who understand. Just search "My Stroke Guide" to find it.

If you'd like help setting up Emergency SOS or Medical ID, just send me a message and I'll do my best to help.

I hope this helps, and please pass it on to anyone who might find it useful.
