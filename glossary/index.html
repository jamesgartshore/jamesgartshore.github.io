Tech words explained

Heard a tech word and not sure what it means? Here are 43 common ones in plain English. You can search, or tap a letter to jump to it.

ABCDEFHILMNOPQRSTUVW

A

Android
The system that runs most non-Apple phones and tablets, such as Samsung, Google Pixel and Motorola.
Antivirus
Software that protects a device from harmful programs. Windows computers already have one built in, called Windows Security or Windows Defender.
App
A program on your phone, tablet or computer that does a particular job, like WhatsApp for messages or the NHS App for prescriptions.
App Store / Google Play
The official places to download apps. iPhones and iPads use the App Store, and Android phones use Google Play. Apps from here are checked for safety first.

B

Backup
A spare copy of your photos, contacts and files, kept somewhere safe like Google Photos or iCloud, so you don't lose them if your device breaks or is lost.
Bluetooth
A way for devices to connect to each other wirelessly over a short distance, such as a phone to wireless headphones, a hearing aid or a car.
Browser
The app you use to visit websites, such as Google Chrome, Safari, Microsoft Edge or Firefox.

C

Cache
Bits of websites and apps your device saves to load them faster next time. Clearing it can fix some problems, and it won't delete your photos or messages.
Cloud
Storing your files on the internet instead of only on your device, so you can reach them from anywhere. Google Drive, iCloud and OneDrive are cloud services.
Cookies
Small files websites save on your device to remember things, like what's in your basket or that you're logged in. The pop-up asking about cookies lets you choose which ones to allow.

D

Data (mobile data)
The internet allowance on your phone contract or SIM, used when you're not on Wi-Fi. It's measured in GB (gigabytes).
Download
Copying something from the internet onto your device, like a photo, a document or an app.

E

Email address
Your address for sending and receiving emails, always in the form name@provider, for example jo.smith@gmail.com.

F

Factory reset
Wiping a device back to how it was when it was new. Everything on it is deleted, so back up first.

H

Hacked
When someone gets into your account or device without permission, often by guessing or stealing a password.

I

Icon
The small picture you tap or click to open an app or do something, like the camera picture that opens the camera.
iOS
The system that runs iPhones. iPads use a version called iPadOS.

L

Link
Words or a button you tap to go to another web page. In emails and texts from people you don't know, links can lead to scam websites.
Log in / sign in
Entering your email or username and password to get into your account on a website or app.

M

Malware
Harmful software that can damage your device, show unwanted adverts or steal information. It often comes from apps or links that aren't from official places.

N

Notification
A message that pops up on your screen from an app, like a new text, an email or a reminder.

O

Operating system
The main software that runs a device and everything on it, such as Windows, macOS, iOS or Android.

P

Password manager
A safe place that remembers all your passwords for you, so you only need to remember one. Google Password Manager and Apple Passwords are built into phones.
Phishing
Fake emails, texts or calls pretending to be from a real company, trying to trick you into giving away passwords, bank details or money.
PIN
A short number code, usually 4 to 6 digits, used to unlock a phone or protect a bank card.

Q

QR code
A square pattern of black and white dots. Point your phone camera at it and it opens a web page, with no typing needed.

R

Router
The box from your broadband provider that creates your home Wi-Fi. Turning it off and on again fixes a lot of internet problems.

S

Screenshot
A picture of whatever is on your screen. Useful for saving information or showing someone a problem.
Settings
The app or menu where you change how your device works, like text size, Wi-Fi, sound and privacy. It usually has a gear icon.
SIM card
The small card in your phone that connects it to your mobile network and holds your phone number.
Smart speaker
A speaker you control by talking to it, such as an Amazon Echo (Alexa) or Google Nest. It can play radio, set timers, answer questions and make calls.
Spam
Unwanted messages, usually adverts or scams, sent to lots of people at once by email, text or phone.
Streaming
Watching or listening to something over the internet without downloading it first, like BBC iPlayer, Netflix or Spotify.

T

Tablet
A device bigger than a phone with a touch screen, like an iPad or a Samsung Galaxy Tab.
Two-step verification
An extra check when you log in, usually a code sent to your phone, so someone with your password still can't get in. Also called two-factor authentication or 2FA.

U

Update
A free improvement to your device or apps from the company that makes them. Updates fix problems and close security holes, so it's best to install them.
Upload
Copying something from your device onto the internet, like sharing a photo on Facebook.
URL / web address
The address of a website, like www.nhs.uk. It appears in the bar at the top of your browser.
Username
The name you use to log in to an account. It's sometimes your email address.

V

Video call
A phone call where you can see each other, using apps like WhatsApp, FaceTime, Zoom or Google Meet.
Virus
A type of harmful program that can spread and damage devices. Pop-ups saying your phone has a virus are almost always scams.
Voice assistant
A helper you talk to, such as Siri on iPhone, Google Assistant on Android or Alexa on Amazon devices.

W

Wi-Fi
Wireless internet in your home or in places like cafes and libraries, so you don't use your mobile data.
