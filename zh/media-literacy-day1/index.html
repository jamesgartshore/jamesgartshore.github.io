Media Literacy Week Day 1

By James Gartshore · 24 October 2026 · Fake news and online info
See the old version of this poster Download the old version

Hi everyone, today is the start of Global Media and Information Literacy Week, which is all about being able to tell what's real and what's not online. With so many fake stories, edited photos and AI-made videos around, it's harder than ever, so I wanted to share some free tools that can help.

Full Fact is the UK's independent fact-checking charity. They look into claims that are going round online, in the news and from politicians, and explain what's true and what isn't. You can search their website at fullfact.org to see if something you've seen has already been checked.

BBC Verify is a team at the BBC that checks viral videos, photos and stories. You'll find their articles on the BBC News website and app, and they often explain exactly how they worked out whether something was real.

Google Lens can help you find out where a photo really came from. Open the Google app, tap the camera icon in the search bar and choose a photo, and it will show you where else that picture appears online. It's a great way to spot an old photo being passed off as new, or a picture taken somewhere completely different.

When you search on Google, you can also tap the three dots next to any result to see "About this result". It tells you more about the website, like how long it's been around and what others say about it, before you click on it.

One of the best tips is a simple one. If a post makes you angry, scared or shocked, pause before you share it. Fake news is designed to make us react quickly without checking.

If you've seen something online and you're not sure whether it's real, just send me a message and I'll do my best to help.
