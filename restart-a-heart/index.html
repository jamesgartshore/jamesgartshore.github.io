Restart a Heart

By James Gartshore · 16 October 2026 · Health and wellbeing
See the old version of this poster Download the old version

Hi everyone, today is Restart a Heart Day, so I wanted to share some free apps and websites that can help you learn CPR and be ready to help if someone's heart stops.

RevivR, from the British Heart Foundation, teaches you CPR in about 15 minutes. You do it on your phone or computer, practising chest compressions on a cushion, and it gives you feedback as you go. Just search "BHF RevivR".

Lifesaver, from Resuscitation Council UK, is a free interactive game that puts you in the middle of real-life emergency scenarios and lets you make the decisions. It's a great way to learn what to do and how to use a defibrillator, and it's suitable for older children too. You can download it as an app or use it at lifesaver.org.uk.

DefibFinder is a free website that shows a map of public defibrillators near you. Just type in your postcode at defibfinder.uk. It's worth having a look to see where your nearest ones are, so you already know if you ever need one. A lot of people worry about using a defibrillator, but they're designed for anyone to use. Once you switch it on, it tells you out loud exactly what to do, and it will only give a shock if the person needs one.

Once you've completed all four Lifesaver scenarios, you can also sign up to the GoodSAM app as a volunteer. It sends you an alert if someone near you has a cardiac arrest, so you can help before the ambulance arrives.

Most importantly, if someone collapses and isn't breathing normally, always call 999 first and put your phone on speaker. The call handler will talk you through CPR step by step and can tell you where the nearest defibrillator is.

If you have 15 minutes spare today, RevivR or Lifesaver are well worth a try. If you'd like help getting started, just send me a message and I'll do my best to help.

I hope this helps, and feel free to pass it on to anyone who might find it useful.
