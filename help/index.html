Get help

Whatever your age or problem, there's free help out there. Pick what fits, and use the official numbers and websites below. My tips are a starting point, but these services can help you directly.

In an emergency, or if someone is in danger

Call 999.

I think I've been scammed

Act quickly, and don't be embarrassed. Scams fool people of all ages.

  • If money or bank details are involved, call your bank now on 159. It connects you safely to most UK banks, any time.
  • Report Fraud (the new name for Action Fraud): reportfraud.police.uk or 0300 123 2040. In Scotland, call Police Scotland on 101.
  • Citizens Advice Scams Action: 0808 250 5050, for advice on what to do next.
  • Scam texts: forward them free to 7726. Scam emails: forward them to report@phishing.gov.uk.
  • If a stranger has had access to your computer, switch it off and change your passwords from a different device.

Related tips: Scams and staying safe

I need someone to help me in person

  • Local tech support groups and drop-ins: many towns have free sessions where volunteers sit down with you. Ask at your library or community centre.
  • Your local library: free computers, Wi-Fi and often help from staff.
  • AbilityNet: free tech help for older and disabled people, by phone or at home. abilitynet.org.uk
  • Age UK Advice Line: 0800 678 1602, for help finding local support.
  • Find free help near you: search "Good Things find a centre" for a map of places that help people get online.

I'm new to computers and the internet

  • Learn My Way: free beginner courses at learnmyway.com.
  • No device or internet? The National Databank gives free mobile data, and libraries have free computers.
  • Tech words explained: what common tech words mean, in plain English.

Sight, hearing or other access needs

  • RNIB Helpline (sight loss): 0303 123 9999
  • RNID (deafness, hearing loss and tinnitus): rnid.org.uk
  • My Computer My Way from AbilityNet: free step-by-step guides to make phones and computers easier to see, hear and use.

Related tips: Health and wellbeing

Children and young people online

  • Internet Matters: guides for parents and grandparents at internetmatters.org.
  • Childline: 0800 1111, free and confidential for anyone under 19.
  • Worried a child is being contacted by someone online? Report it to CEOP at ceop.police.uk.

Money and bills

  • MoneyHelper: free, government-backed money guidance. 0800 138 7777, moneyhelper.org.uk
  • Turn2us Benefits Calculator: check what you could be entitled to at turn2us.org.uk.
  • Citizens Advice: free help with debt, benefits and bills at citizensadvice.org.uk.

Related tips: Money

A problem with something I bought

  • Citizens Advice consumer helpline: 0808 223 1133, for refunds, faulty goods and problems with online shopping.
  • If you paid by card and the seller won't help, contact your bank. Card payments often give you extra protection.

Feeling low or lonely

  • Samaritans: 116 123, free, any time, day or night.
  • Shout: text SHOUT to 85258 if you'd rather text than talk.
  • The Silver Line (for older people): 0800 4 70 80 90, a free friendly chat, day or night.

I'm a volunteer sharing tips in my own time, so I can't fix devices or give personal advice, but I'm happy to point you in the right direction. Message me on Nextdoor if you're not sure where to start.
