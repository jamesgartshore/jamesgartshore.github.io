Sore hands? Let your phone and laptop do the work

By James Gartshore · 12 October 2026

Hi everyone, today is World Arthritis Day, so I wanted to share some features on your phone and laptop that can make life a lot easier if you have sore, stiff or painful hands.

Typing on a small screen can be really uncomfortable, but you don't have to. Voice typing lets you speak your messages, emails and searches, and your phone types them out for you. On a phone, just tap the small microphone on the keyboard and start talking. On a Windows laptop, press the Windows key and H together, and the same thing works in almost any program.

You can also let your phone do things for you without touching the screen at all. On an Android phone, say "Hey Google", and on an iPhone, say "Siri" or "Hey Siri", followed by what you want, like "call Sarah", "send a text to John" or "remind me to take my tablets at 6pm". If it doesn't respond, you may need to switch it on first in your phone's settings.

Making text and buttons bigger can help too, because it's easier to read and much easier to tap the right thing first time. On a phone, go to Settings, then Display, and look for the text or font size option. On a Windows laptop, go to Settings, then Accessibility, then Text size.

If tapping in a PIN or password is fiddly, you can unlock your phone with your face or fingerprint instead. You'll find this in your phone's Settings under Security, or Face ID on an iPhone. Many newer Windows laptops can do the same, which you can set up in Settings, then Accounts, then Sign-in options.

All of these are free and already built into your devices, so there's nothing extra to buy or download.

If you'd like help setting any of these up, just comment below and I'll do my best to help.

I hope this helps, and feel free to pass it on to anyone who might find it useful.
